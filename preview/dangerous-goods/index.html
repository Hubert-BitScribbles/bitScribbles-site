    Where it started

    At a ferry ticket booth. The attendant asked whether there were any dangerous goods on board, and the answer was "Well, only me."

    That's the idea. The dangerous good isn't a thing, it's the person carrying it. The danger might be a challenge to how things are done, a social issue, or something personal that's frightening at first. The good is the intention, the new start, the benefit at the end.

    Real dangerous goods rules don't ban anything. They exist so risky things can travel: declare what it is, pack it properly, and tell the people handling it what to expect. The app works the same way.

    Journey before outcome

    It isn't a blank diary. Each thing you carry gets its own manifest: what it is, the danger, the good, and handling notes. Then it travels. Short entries mark how it's moving, until it reaches one of three endings, shown exactly alike, because none of them is a failure.

    Three stages

    1. A private journal. Everything stays on the phone: no accounts, no server, and it works offline, like the other bitScribbles apps.

    2. Share your manifest. Hand one manifest to someone you trust, as a card sent through your own messages app. The app holds nothing that's shared.

    3. Recruit your crew. A few people sign on for one voyage: a support crew for a hard journey, or a shared voyage for something you're doing together. This needs a server, so the promise becomes "only your crew can read it", with end-to-end encryption so bitScribbles can't read it either.

    Safe by design

    People will write down what they feel most exposed about, so the safety thinking comes first, not last.

    • Social as a gesture, not a network: no feed, no followers, no likes or comments.
    • A crew is for one voyage. The person who declared it invites and removes, anyone can leave quietly, and there are no read receipts or "seen" marks.
    • Asking is gentle and can always be declined. No streaks.
    • A lock, a discreet name and icon, and a quick way to hide or erase, for anyone whose phone gets checked.
    • Help lines reachable from every manifest, and an age minimum for anything social.
